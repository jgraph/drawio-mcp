## Design for the chat view (read this first too)

When the diagram is shown inline in the chat, it gets about 800 px of width and at most 800 px of height, and it is scaled down until the whole diagram fits. A diagram drawn larger than that shrinks, and its text shrinks with it. Design for that frame:

- **Size:** keep the diagram within about 5 grid columns and 6 rows (about 900 × 800 px). If the content needs more, keep every step and element the user asked for and use the larger grid with `fontSize=16` described below, or draw two diagrams. Explanations can go into a note or your text answer.
- **Text:** put `fontSize=14` in the style of every shape (the default 12 becomes too small once scaled), keep edge labels at the default 12, and start with a title: a `text` cell at the top with `fontSize=20;fontStyle=1`.
- **Labels:** a short name of 2–5 words per shape. Where it helps the reader, add the key detail as a second line in a smaller font: the technology, role, protocol or condition, escaped like every HTML label (`value="Order Service&lt;br&gt;&lt;small&gt;Node.js · REST&lt;/small&gt;"`). Edge labels say what flows or why, in 1–3 words (`HTTPS`, `OrderPlaced`, `reads`, `No`); color the labels of decision branches, `fontColor=#2D7600` for yes and success, `fontColor=#B20000` for no and failure. Longer explanations go into a note shape or your text answer, not into the label.
- **Box size:** fit each box to its text, about 20 px per line plus 20 px; a box much larger than its text looks empty. Boxes of one kind still share one size: the size of the fullest one.
- **Complete content:** draw every element, path and relation the request names, plus the obvious companions a reader expects: end terminals on every path, error, cancel and retry paths with their counter or limit, the actors at both ends of a flow, and a one-line legend for line styles and colors.
- **Groups:** show tiers, zones, stages, systems and namespaces as pale titled containers with their members inside, not as loose labels beside them.
- **Color by role:** the same color for the same kind of element, from draw.io's palette (`fillColor`/`strokeColor`): blue `#dae8fc`/`#6c8ebf` for main steps and services, green `#d5e8d4`/`#82b366` for success, outputs and data, yellow `#fff2cc`/`#d6b656` for decisions and notes, orange `#ffe6cc`/`#d79b00` for external systems and waiting, red `#f8cecc`/`#b85450` for errors and cancellation, purple `#e1d5e7`/`#9673a6` for security and special steps. Keep containers and lanes pale (`#f5f5f5` or a light tint).
- **Consistency:** one visual language per diagram. Take all icons from one set (one library such as AWS 4 or Cisco 19, or the set named in brackets in a `search_shapes` title) and give them one size; one-color icons get one `fillColor`. Shapes of one kind share size, corner rounding, outline width and font. Connectors share one color, width, arrowhead and path style that match the shape outlines (the same `strokeWidth`, a `strokeColor` from the same palette). Change a color or line only for something that means something different, and name it in the legend.
- **Icons:** an icon's label sits below it. Leave a full grid row between rows of icons so the labels have room; an edge that meets an icon from below ends under its label.

## Laying out diagrams for the chat view

You place every shape yourself; there is no layout pass (don't set `postLayout`). Aim for a compact landscape grid, short straight edges, and no edge crossing a shape. Pick the pattern for the diagram type below.

- **Grid:** the rigid grid below (columns 180 px apart, rows 120 px apart). Up to 12 shapes: at most 4 columns × 5 rows. 13–30 shapes: at most 6 columns × 6 rows, with `fontSize=16` on every shape and `fontSize=14` on edge labels, so the text stays readable when the diagram is shown at about 70 %.
- **Flowcharts:** the main path runs down one column. A branch goes into the next column at the row of its decision and continues down there. A main path longer than 5 rows snakes: continue at the top of the next free column. Decisions are `rhombus;whiteSpace=wrap;html=1;` 160×90 with a question of at most 4 words. Put a loop's target in the same column as its source, so the loop-back edge is a short line along the side. Branches join only where the process really merges, never through a step on another branch's way. Every path ends at an end terminal.
- **Pipelines and parallel steps:** a linear pipeline (CI/CD, ETL, approval chain) runs left to right in one row and continues in the row below when it is longer than 5 steps. Steps that run in parallel stack in one column, with the step before them to the left of the column's middle and the step after them to the right, so every edge is its own short line. When the user names stages (build, test, deploy), put each stage's steps in a pale titled container (`swimlane;startSize=26;html=1;` with the stage's tint as `fillColor`).
- **Architecture, cloud and service diagrams:** one row per tier, top to bottom: clients, entry (CDN, load balancer, API gateway), services, data. A component that every member of the next row connects to (API gateway, load balancer, message queue or event bus) is a wide bar, 50 px tall, spanning the row it serves, so each connection is a short vertical line. Each service's own database or storage sits directly below the service; a message queue or event bus that the services share goes in the row below their databases (or beside them), never between a service and its own database. Asynchronous and event links are `dashed=1`. In cloud group containers (VPC, subnet, availability zone, auto scaling group), the title sits at the top-left or under a centered top icon: leave 50 px free at the top before the first child.
- **Network diagrams:** a tree from the top: internet, firewall, router, the switches in one row, and each switch's devices in the row below it, centered under their switch. Wireless clients hang below the access point. Device icons are 50×50 with their label below. Links are straight cable lines without arrows (`endArrow=none;html=1;`, no `edgeStyle`), `dashed=1` for wireless.
- **Org charts:** the root centered at the top; each manager's direct reports in the row below, centered under their manager. A manager with more than 3 reports stacks them in one column below, offset 40 px to the right, connected from the manager's left side. Reporting lines have no arrowheads (`edgeStyle=orthogonalEdgeStyle;endArrow=none;html=1;`).
- **ER schemas:** one table per entity, a `swimlane;fontStyle=1;childLayout=stackLayout;horizontalStack=0;resizeParent=1;resizeParentMax=0;horizontal=1;startSize=26;collapsible=0;html=1;` container with one child per column: `text;strokeColor=none;fillColor=none;align=left;verticalAlign=middle;spacingLeft=4;html=1;` at `y = 26 + i × 26`, height 26, the table's width, with values like `PK id` or `FK book_id`. Tables are 200 px wide; put them in every other grid column (x = 40, 400, 760), so a free column of 160 px separates tables side by side, and stack a table that references another under it where you can (a free row between them), so the relation is a short straight line. At most 3 tables per row. A table that many others reference (users, books, orders) goes in the middle, with the tables that reference it on both sides, so its relations leave from both sides instead of sharing one line. A relationship edge connects the foreign-key row to the primary-key row (the row cells' ids as `source` and `target`), so the line meets each table at that row: `edgeStyle=entityRelationEdgeStyle;html=1;` plus the cardinality arrows (`startArrow=ERmandOne;endArrow=ERmany;`, or `ERone`, `ERzeroToOne`, `ERzeroToMany`, `ERoneToMany`). No edge labels; the arrows say it.
- **UML class diagrams:** each class is a table like the ER one: a `swimlane;fontStyle=1;childLayout=stackLayout;horizontalStack=0;resizeParent=1;resizeParentMax=0;horizontal=1;startSize=26;collapsible=0;html=1;` container with attribute rows (`text;strokeColor=none;fillColor=none;align=left;verticalAlign=middle;spacingLeft=4;html=1;`, `- name: String`), then a separator `line;strokeWidth=1;fillColor=none;align=left;verticalAlign=middle;spacingTop=-1;spacingLeft=3;spacingRight=3;rotatable=0;labelPosition=right;points=[];portConstraint=eastwest;html=1;` 8 px tall, then method rows (`+ total(): Decimal`). At most 3 classes per row; a superclass above its subclasses; classes linked by an association with multiplicities side by side, so the line runs horizontally and its end labels sit above it, clear of the class text. Inheritance `endArrow=block;endFill=0;`, composition `startArrow=diamondThin;startFill=1;startSize=14;endArrow=none;` (the diamond at the whole), aggregation the same with `startFill=0`, association `endArrow=open;` or `endArrow=none;`, all `edgeStyle=orthogonalEdgeStyle;html=1;`. Every association and composition shows both multiplicities (`1`, `0..*`, `1..*`) as two child labels of the edge, at its ends: `<mxCell id="m1" value="1" style="edgeLabel;resizable=0;html=1;align=left;verticalAlign=bottom;" vertex="1" connectable="0" parent="EDGE_ID"><mxGeometry x="-1" relative="1" as="geometry"/></mxCell>` and the same with `x="1"` and `align=right` for the target end.
- **State diagrams:** the initial state `ellipse;fillColor=#000000;strokeColor=none;` 30×30, the final state `ellipse;shape=endState;fillColor=#000000;` 30×30, states `rounded=1;arcSize=40;whiteSpace=wrap;html=1;` 140×50. The main path runs left to right in one row (continue in the row below after 5 states); states that end or leave the main path (cancelled, failed, refunded) go in the row below, under the state they are reached from. Transitions are straight arrows (`endArrow=open;endSize=10;html=1;`, no `edgeStyle`) labelled with the event, 1–3 words; place the states so that no straight transition passes through another state.
- **Use case diagrams:** the system boundary is a pale titled container; the use cases are ellipses 160×60 in one column inside it (two columns for more than 6). The primary actor (`shape=umlActor;verticalLabelPosition=bottom;verticalAlign=top;html=1;` 30×60) stands left of the boundary at the middle of its use cases, secondary actors (systems, technicians) right of it. An included or extending use case goes in a second column inside the boundary, beside the use cases that use it. Actor links are straight lines without arrows (`endArrow=none;html=1;`, no `edgeStyle`); include and extend are `dashed=1;endArrow=open;html=1;` with the value `«include»` or `«extend»`. When several use cases include the same one, label only one of those lines, or none and add a one-line legend below the boundary.
- **Mind maps:** the central topic in the middle (`ellipse;fontSize=18;fontStyle=1;` 180×80); the main branches split evenly to its left and right, in one column per side 220 px from the center, spread over the height; each branch's items stacked in a column further out (140×36, rows 46 px apart), centered on their branch. One color per branch, used for its items and links. Links: `edgeStyle=entityRelationEdgeStyle;startArrow=none;endArrow=none;segment=10;curved=1;html=1;` with the branch's `strokeColor`.
- **Timelines:** events read left to right in rows of at most 4 (columns 200 px apart); the next row continues right to left under the last event (a snake), joined by one connector down the side. Each event is a small rounded date badge (`rounded=1;arcSize=50;fontStyle=1;` 100×36) on the row's axis, connected badge to badge with arrows, with an event card (160×60, `whiteSpace=wrap`) under its badge. Color the eras or themes, with a one-line legend.
- **Decision trees:** questions are `rhombus` 160×80, answers are 1-word edge labels, recommendations are rounded boxes. When each "no" leads to the next question, stack the questions in one column with each "yes" leading to the recommendation beside it; otherwise grow the tree downwards and put the recommendations in the bottom row.
- **Fault and attack trees:** top-down, the top event at the top. Each event is a box 160×50 with its gate directly below it, touching, pointing up: AND `shape=or;direction=north;`, OR `shape=xor;direction=north;` (draw.io's names), 60×50, labelled `AND` or `OR`. The gate's inputs stand in the row below, centered under it; basic causes are `ellipse` 140×50. Connect each input to its gate with `edgeStyle=orthogonalEdgeStyle;endArrow=none;html=1;exitX=0.5;exitY=0;entryX=0;entryY=0.5;` (the input's top to the gate's flat side, as rotated); a gate touches its event, with no line between them.
- **Server racks:** the rack cabinet from `search_shapes` is a container with `numDisp=descend`, which numbers U1 at the bottom like a real rack; name positions with those numbers. Its devices are children at `x` = its `marginLeft` and `y` = `marginTop` + (units above the device) × `rackUnitSize`, as tall as their units × `rackUnitSize`; one-line labels (`fontSize=12`) to the right of the rack, since a unit is only 15 px tall.
- **Scenes** (a water cycle, a production line, a supply chain): the objects as icons from `search_shapes` (sun, cloud, factory, truck), 64–80 px with their name below, placed where they are in the scene, and the steps as numbered labels on curved arrows (`curved=1`) between them; not landscapes built from rectangles and triangles.
- **Short edges:** connected shapes sit in neighbouring grid cells; a shape with many connections goes in the middle of the shapes it connects. An edge that has to cross two or more cells, or run around other shapes, means a shape is in the wrong place.
- **Edges:** connect neighbours side to side. Edges leaving one shape should leave from different sides or from different `exitX`/`exitY` points, so their labels don't pile up. A labeled edge between two shapes side by side needs room for its label: one word between neighbouring columns, more only with a free column between the shapes. Set `routing: "libavoid"` only when an edge would otherwise cross a shape; the viewer routes it anyway when a right-angle edge would cross one. Routing reroutes only the right-angle edges; straight and curved lines stay as drawn.

## Changing a diagram

The user can ask for changes to the diagram in the chat. Start from the current diagram: the XML you sent last, or the diagram as the viewer shows it when that was sent back to you. Keep the ids, positions and text of everything the request doesn't touch, and send the whole diagram again.

- **A look** (shadows, rounded corners, hand-drawn, a color theme, a font, a connector style): apply it to every cell it concerns; see **Look and feel** in this reference for the keys.
- **Icons for the steps:** look up each icon with `search_shapes` and take them all from one set, give them one size of 48–64 px, put the step's name below it (`verticalLabelPosition=bottom;verticalAlign=top`) and add a free row below each row of icons for the names.
- **More content:** place new shapes on free grid cells next to what they connect to, and move the others aside rather than letting a connector cross them.
- **Images:** use only image URLs that `search_shapes` returned. Other images on the web don't display in the chat.

## Choosing a look

Use the classic colors unless the request says where the diagram goes; then use the fitting theme from **Color themes** right away: a slide, report or board meeting → professional; printing → minimal; a rough idea, sketch or whiteboard → hand-drawn; a company's or product's color → brand; a dark dashboard or dark slides → dark.

After the first diagram in a conversation, end your reply with one short offer that fits it, in plain words and without naming style keys: official icons for an architecture, cloud or network diagram drawn with plain boxes; swimlanes for a process with several roles; a cleaner, corporate look for slides or documents; a hand-drawn look for an early idea. Offer once per conversation. When the user chose a look or theme, don't offer another one; offer content (icons, swimlanes, a missing path) or nothing.
